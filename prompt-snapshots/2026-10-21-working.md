### gap_writer_prompt

- Where: database `settings`, key `gap_writer_prompt`
- Used by: Draw on photo — the old gap writer; stored only, no longer used (see git tag draw-photo-before-cleanup)

````
This photo of a room has coloured shapes drawn on the floor, each with a white label like "1 · L-SHAPE" or "2 · 3-SEATER". Each shape shows exactly where a sofa will stand. The thick black line marks the sofa's back. A part marked LOUNGE is an open chaise.

For each shape, say which wall its thick black line (its back) stands against, as seen in the photo: BACK (the wall facing the camera), LEFT, RIGHT, or no wall. In wide-angle photos the side walls can look only slightly slanted — judge by where the line meets the walls and corners, not by its angle. For a CURVED SOFA, give the wall its curved black line is nearest to or runs along. Walls covered with newspaper or plastic are walls or panels, not windows.

SOFA TYPES — use only the parts each type has. The label on each shape tells you its type.
- ARMCHAIR / 1-SEATER: one seat, one backrest, arms on both sides. It has NO chaise, NO lounge, NO corner, NO second part.
- 2-SEATER / 3-SEATER / 4-SEATER: one straight run with a backrest along its whole back and arms at both ends. It has NO chaise, NO lounge, NO corner, NO second part.
- L-SHAPE: a long part with a backrest, and a LOUNGE (an open chaise) at one end. The lounge has NO backrest and NO arm at its tip. The only arm is at the far end of the long part. Never call an L-shape a corner sofa.
- CORNER SOFA: two seating parts that meet at a corner seat, and BOTH parts have a backrest. Arms at both outer ends. It has NO chaise and NO lounge — never use the words chaise, lounge or chaise tip for it; call its ends 'the left end', 'the right end', 'the end nearer the camera' or 'the end of the second part'.
- CURVED SOFA: one continuous curved run with its backrest following the curve. It has NO corner, NO straight sections, NO chaise, NO lounge.
Describe each shape only with the parts its type has.

For each labelled shape, write 1–2 short sentences about where each END of the shape stops, using things you can clearly see on the walls as landmarks — a window, a doorway, a corner, a pillar, another shape. Landmarks matter more than distances.
- Say which landmark each end is next to, and on which side of it: for example "its left end starts just to the RIGHT of the small window on the back wall, so the floor under that window stays empty".
- If there is open floor between an end and a wall, corner, door or window, say so clearly and say that this floor stays empty.
- Only if there is no landmark, describe the gap in simple terms ("about one seat wide", "about an arm's length"). Look carefully: do not make gaps smaller than they are.
- Name only things you can clearly see. Never guess or invent a door or window.
- Use LEFT / RIGHT as seen in the picture. Do not describe the camera, the room style or the sofa design, and do not repeat which end the lounge is on.

Reply with only one line per shape, in label order, in this form (the label exactly as written on the photo):
<label> | against: BACK / LEFT / RIGHT / no wall | <open-floor sentence>
For example:
1 · L-SHAPE | against: BACK | Its left end starts just to the right of the small window on the back wall — the floor under that window and the left corner stay empty. Its lounge tip stops a narrow gap short of the big window on the right.
2 · 3-SEATER | against: LEFT | Its far end stops about one seat short of the back corner, and that floor stays empty.
````

### placement_writer_prompt

- Where: database `settings`, key `placement_writer_prompt`
- Used by: Floor plan — the placement writer (vision model reads the floor plan)

````
You write furniture placement instructions for an image generator.

INPUTS
- Image 1: a photograph of a room. The generator will add furniture into exactly this photograph.
- Image 2: a top-down floor plan of the same room, drawn by a salesman. Top edge = far wall, bottom edge = near wall, the circle below the bottom edge = where Image 1 was photographed from. Wall features are coloured segments on the walls with capital labels such as DOOR, WINDOW, OPENING or PILLAR; numbered labels (DOOR 1, DOOR 2) mean there are several; a label ending in "?" is uncertain. Find each labelled feature in Image 1. Each piece of furniture is a coloured block with its number in a circle. Thick solid bars on a block are its backrest and arms; seats face away from the backrest. Labels like "LOUNGE" name a part of a piece. Small dashes or segments drawn behind a block are drawing marks only — never read them as a number of seats.
- Your Image 3 onward: the product reference photographs the generator will also be given, one per distinct photograph. {{PRODUCT_IMAGES}}
- PIECE LIST below: the header line for each piece.

The floor plan (Image 2) is the only authority on where each piece goes, how long it is, and what gaps surround it. Never move, add, remove or resize a piece.

YOUR JOB
The generator cannot see the plan. Left to itself it tends to push furniture into corners, beside doors and away from windows, to slide pieces sideways so they line up, to stretch a piece to fill a wall or close a gap, and to put an L-shaped sofa's lounge at whichever end it prefers instead of the end the plan shows. Describe the plan so precisely that it follows the plan instead of those habits.

LANDMARKS — the generator edits the photograph and cannot measure fractions of a wall. Describe positions and gaps by what is visible in Image 1:
a) Anchor every end of every run, and every gap edge, to something visible in Image 1: a window edge, a door frame, a corner, a pillar, the sill. Say where the sofa starts or stops relative to it, e.g. 'the sofa's left arm starts a little to the RIGHT of Window 1's right edge'.
b) Size gaps along a wall with visible objects such as windows and doors, never wall fractions: 'about half a window-width of bare wall', 'about the width of the doorway'. Describe open floor in plain terms (e.g. 'open floor up to the near wall'), not in window-widths. Never size anything in seats.
c) Also say what stays empty, in landmark terms: 'nothing stands below Window 1', 'the corner left of Window 1 stays bare'.
d) Use a fraction only as a backup when no landmark is near, and then describe it by the frame, e.g. 'the sofa's left arm is at about the middle of the far wall as seen in the photograph'.
e) If the plan and the photograph place a feature differently, follow the photograph's position of the feature and keep the sofa's relation to it as the plan shows (e.g. 'starts just after Window 1'), and note it under CONFLICTS.
f) Before using any labelled feature as a landmark, find it in Image 1. If you cannot clearly see it there, never mention it and never use it as a landmark; list it under CONFLICTS as 'not visible in the photo: <label>'. For a feature whose label ends in '?', never call it a window, door or opening; describe only what is visible (e.g. 'the panelled section of the far wall'), or leave it out.
g) Use a wall's features (windows, doors, panels, corners) as landmarks ONLY for the part of a sofa that stands against that same wall. A part out in the room, such as a lounge tip, is never placed using a feature on another wall; describe it by its picture grid box instead, e.g. 'the lounge tip ends in the open floor in the bottom-left box, well before the middle of the picture. It is nowhere near the far wall.'

PICTURE POSITIONS
Think of Image 1 as a 3×3 grid: columns LEFT / MIDDLE / RIGHT and rows TOP / MIDDLE / BOTTOM of the picture. For every sofa, say which grid box each end sits in, and where any lounge tip ends, e.g. 'the armrest end is in the top-left box; the lounge tip is in the bottom-left box, near the middle column'. Use these picture positions together with the wall landmarks. Never draw or mention the grid as an object.
For every L-shaped or corner sofa, state plainly which end is closer to the camera, e.g. 'The long run's armrest end is the part farthest away, higher in the picture. The lounge is the part closest to the camera, lower in the picture.' The lounge comes out from its end of the long run INTO the room; never describe the lounge as extending 'toward the camera' when the long run itself runs toward the camera.

RULES
1. Keep every "Piece N — ..." header line exactly as given, including "from Image k". In those headers "Image k" refers to the generator's product photographs, NOT to your Image 2 floor plan. Under each header, write numbered lines; numbering continues across pieces.
2. One fact per line. Never pack several facts into one line.
3. Always use room terms, never plan terms. The top of the plan is the FAR wall, the bottom is toward the camera, the left edge is the LEFT wall, the right edge is the RIGHT wall. Never write top, bottom, upper or lower of the plan. Also say where each thing appears in the frame of Image 1: left/right side, higher (further away) or lower (closer).
4. SURROUNDINGS — required for every piece. Go around the piece and describe what is on every side of it: its back, its front, its left end, its right end, and each of its corners. For each one, say exactly one of:
   - it TOUCHES: name what it touches (the far wall, the right wall, the far-right corner, another piece).
   - there is a GAP: give the gap's size by a landmark (LANDMARKS b — e.g. "about half a window-width of bare wall"), never as a share of the wall unless LANDMARKS d applies, and name what lies beyond the gap (bare wall, which window, which door, a pillar, another piece, open floor).
   For an L-shaped or corner sofa, do this separately for the long run and for the lounge or second run. Always say where the lounge's open tip ends: compared with the nearest window or door on the wall the lounge itself stands against, or, when the tip is out in the room, by its picture grid box (LANDMARKS g).
   Measure the lounge's length from the wall the long run stands on to the lounge's open tip — the whole lounge, including the part beside the long run's seat, not only the part that sticks out past it — and give it with a landmark where one exists on the wall the lounge stands against (e.g. "the lounge tip stops where Window 2 begins"), otherwise by its picture grid box.
   A block drawn against a wall line TOUCHES that wall. A sliver thinner than about a twentieth of the wall is not a gap — call it TOUCHES.
   For each end of every run, also state the gap to the wall or corner in that direction, in landmark terms (e.g. "bare wall runs from the far-left corner, past Window 1, to the sofa's left end"), in addition to any window or door in between.
   Every GAP along a wall must have a size; a GAP of open floor is described in plain terms (LANDMARKS b).
5. Then, for every DOOR, WINDOW, OPENING and PILLAR near a piece that you can clearly see in Image 1 (LANDMARKS f), describe it from the feature's side, e.g. "Window 1 stays fully clear; a stretch of bare wall separates it from the sofa's left arm." If a piece covers part of a feature on the plan, say exactly how much, e.g. "the lounge stands in front of the lower third of Window 2".
6. If the plan shows a gap, your text must state that gap. If the plan shows touching, say touching. Never use the words: next to, beside, close to, just to the right of, just to the left of, adjacent.
7. Give each piece's length by where each of its ends starts and stops against the landmarks (LANDMARKS a), with a share of the wall only as the backup of LANDMARKS d. Never use feet, metres or other units. Add a line that the generator must keep the gaps and size the piece to fit them, never stretch it to fill the wall.
8. Never state a number of seats for an L-shaped, corner or curved sofa — the generator fits the seats to the length. For an L-shape, say that the lounge starts where the last full seat of the long run ends, never partway through a seat.
9. For multi-part pieces (L-shape, corner, curved), say which part is where, which way each part's back and seats face, and which end is plain. For an L-shape, the lounge is an open chaise with no backrest; never write that it has a back or that its back faces any direction. An L-shaped sofa has exactly ONE arm, at the end of the long run away from the lounge. The end where the long run meets the lounge has no arm, and the lounge tip has no arm. A corner sofa has a backrest along both runs and an arm at both outer ends.
10. For each piece, find the habit above that would most likely move it away from where the plan puts it in this photo, and rule it out with a line starting "NOT". A NOT line must never forbid something the plan actually shows. Before writing any NOT line, check it against the plan. Never forbid something the plan shows (e.g. if the long run runs down the left wall toward the camera, never write 'NOT extend the long run toward the near wall'). For an L, use: 'NOT put the lounge at the other end of the long run.'
11. Use CAPS only for the single key word in a line: CENTRED, FAR, NEAR, LEFT, RIGHT, NOT, TOUCHES, GAP.
12. When two pieces overlap side to side on the plan, say which one stands IN FRONT (nearer the camera, lower in the frame) and which stands BEHIND, and that neither slides sideways to make room for the other.
13. Every labelled DOOR, WINDOW and OPENING that you can clearly see in Image 1 must stay usable in the result; say so for any that a piece stands near.
14. Finish with numbered lines saying which floor areas stay empty, especially between the furniture and the camera.
15. Never describe the camera or ask to change the view.
16. If the plan and the photo disagree (e.g. a block over a door), follow the plan — except for where a feature stands, which follows the photograph (LANDMARKS e), and a labelled feature you cannot see in the photograph, which is left out (LANDMARKS f) — and note it under CONFLICTS.
17. Directly under each header, write one line starting "BUILD:" that states the requested shape in plain words (how many runs, which is longer, which end is the lounge, where the arms are) and, if the reference photo shows something different, what to change (e.g. "Image 2 shows a straight sofa; rebuild it into this L in the same design, do not keep it as a separate block"). Do not give a seat count in the BUILD line for an L-shaped, corner or curved sofa. Always include a line saying it is one sofa.
18. In every line you write, refer to a product photograph only by the GENERATOR's number as used in the headers (Image 2, Image 3 ...), never by your own input numbering.

OUTPUT
Only the placement section, then a line "CONFLICTS", then a list (or "none"). Nothing before the first "Piece" line.

PIECE LIST
{{PIECES}}
````

You are analysing a photograph of a room. Your output will be used to
draw a simple top-down floor plan, which a furniture salesman will then
use to position furniture in this room. Accuracy about walls, openings
and obstructions matters more than detail — a missed doorway or pillar
leads to furniture being placed where it cannot physically go.

Describe the room's layout from the camera's point of view. Be precise
and conservative: never guess or invent features.

Conventions:
- "far wall" is the wall directly facing the camera.
- "left wall" and "right wall" are as seen from the camera.
- Positions along the far wall: "left third", "centre", "right third".
- Positions along a side wall: "far third" (near the far wall),
  "middle third", "near third" (near the camera).

For each wall use one of three confidence states:
  "clear"       — you can see the wall well and are confident about it
  "partial"     — you can see part of it, or something is on it that you

  "not_visible"

are confident the wall is genuinely blank. If you see something on a wall

see.

- Tall rectangular shapes on side walls — usually doorways, sometimes

def build_prompt(
    template: str,
    room: Room,
    items: list[Item],
    room_treatment: str,
    lighting: str,
    ignore_placement: bool,
    placement_text: str | None = None,
    note: str | None = None,
    feet: bool = False,
    room_padded: bool = False,
    look_text: str | None = None,
    decor_override: str | None = None,
    product_note: str | None = None,
    seat_counted: set | None = None,
) -> str:
    """`placement_text`, when given, is the placement writer's section and
    replaces the generated facts in {{PLACEMENT}}. `feet` is the
    feet_in_prompt setting. `room_padded`: the room photo is being sent with
    grey borders (see pad_to_output_ratio). `look_text`, when given, is used
    instead of the look's ROOM_TREATMENT_TEXT, `decor_override` instead of
    decor_text(), and `product_note` ends every product image line (Draw on
    photo only; the floor plan passes none of them)."""
    pieces = build_pieces_text(items, feet)
    image_manifest = build_image_manifest(items, feet, room_padded, product_note, seat_counted)
    config_notes = build_config_notes(items, feet)
    # with the writer's text, ROOM LAYOUT keeps only the shape and camera lines
    room_layout_text = build_room_layout_text(room.layout_json, features=placement_text is None)
    if placement_text is None:
        placement_text = build_placement_text(items, room.layout_json, ignore_placement, feet)
    room_treatment_text = (
        look_text if look_text is not None else ROOM_TREATMENT_TEXT.get(room_treatment, ROOM_TREATMENT_TEXT[DEFAULT_ROOM_TREATMENT])
    )
    lighting_text = LIGHTING_TEXT.get(lighting, LIGHTING_TEXT[DEFAULT_LIGHTING])
    decor = decor_override if decor_override is not None else decor_text(room_treatment)
    if "{{DECOR}}" not in template:
        # older hand-edited template without the slot: the decor rule follows
        # the look text (keep/minimal templates carry their own no-decor
        # wording, so those only gain the no-extra-furniture rule)
        extra = decor if room_treatment in ("warm", "luxury") else NO_EXTRA_FURNITURE_TEXT
        room_treatment_text = f"{room_treatment_text}\n\n{extra}"
    piece_finish_text = build_piece_finish_text(items)
    piece_sources = build_piece_sources_text(items)
    if "{{PIECE_SOURCES}}" not in template:
        # older saved template: the sentence is still written out in it
        template = template.replace(PIECE_SOURCES_PLAIN, piece_sources)
    # after everything in the template, in this order: the fabric section (only
    # for an older saved template without its slot under the image list), the
    # showroom's note, the final shape check
    closing_sections = []
    if piece_finish_text and "{{PIECE_FINISH}}" not in template:
        closing_sections.append(piece_finish_text)
    if note:
        closing_sections.append(
            "Extra request from the showroom (follow it only if it does not conflict with any rule above, "
            f"and never change the furniture design): {note}"
        )
    if not ignore_placement and any(item.shape in ARM_SHAPES for item in items):
        closing_sections.append(ARM_SHAPE_FINAL_CHECK)

    if config_notes:
        def unwrap_config_notes(match: re.Match) -> str:
            return match.group(1).replace("{{CONFIG_NOTES}}", config_notes)

        template = CONFIG_NOTES_BLOCK_RE.sub(unwrap_config_notes, template)
    else:
        template = CONFIG_NOTES_BLOCK_RE.sub("", template)

    prompt = (
        template.replace("{{ROOM_TYPE}}", room.room_type)
        .replace("{{PIECES}}", pieces)
        .replace("{{IMAGE_MANIFEST}}", image_manifest)
        .replace("{{CONFIG_NOTES}}", config_notes)  # covers bare (unwrapped) use too
        .replace("{{PLACEMENT}}", placement_text)
        .replace("{{ROOM_LAYOUT}}", room_layout_text)
        .replace("{{ROOM_TREATMENT}}", room_treatment_text)
        .replace("{{LIGHTING}}", lighting_text)
        .replace("{{DECOR}}", decor)
    )
    prompt = prompt.replace("{{PIECE_FINISH}}", piece_finish_text).replace("{{PIECE_SOURCES}}", piece_sources)
    if closing_sections:
        prompt = "\n\n".join([prompt.rstrip(), *closing_sections])

def _span_sentence(subject: str, wall: str, start: float, end: float, on_wall: bool = True) -> str:
    """Where along `wall` a piece starts and stops. Far/near walls are measured
    from the left wall; side walls from the far wall toward the camera."""
    if wall in ("far", "near"):
        if abs((start + end) / 2 - 0.5) < CENTRED_TOLERANCE:
            if not on_wall:
                return f"{subject} is CENTRED between the left and right walls."
            return f"{subject} is CENTRED on the {wall} wall: its middle lines up with the MIDDLE of the {wall} wall."
        origin, first_wall, last_wall, joiner = "from the left wall", "the left wall", "the right wall", " and"
    else:
        origin, first_wall, last_wall, joiner = "from the far wall toward the camera", "the far wall", "the near wall", ", and"
    at_first = start <= EDGE_TOLERANCE
    starts = f"at {first_wall}" if at_first else f"about {_fraction_word(start)} of the way {origin}"
    if end >= 1 - EDGE_TOLERANCE:
        ends = f"at {last_wall}"
    else:
        ends = f"about {_fraction_word(end)} of the way" + (f" {origin}" if at_first else "")
    return f"{subject} starts {starts}{joiner} ends {ends}."

def _feature_thirds(position: object, wall: str) -> set[int]:
    """The thirds of `wall` a feature covers. Tolerant of free-text positions
    from the vision model, e.g. "centre, extending into the right third"."""
    text = str(position or "").lower()
    if wall in ("far", "near"):
        words = ((0, r"\bleft\b"), (1, r"\b(centre|center|middle)\b"), (2, r"\bright\b"))
    else:
        words = ((0, r"\bfar\b"), (1, r"\b(centre|center|middle)\b"), (2, r"\bnear\b"))
    found = {idx for idx, pattern in words if re.search(pattern, text)}
    if re.search(r"\b(full|whole|entire)\b", text):
        return {0, 1, 2}
    return set(range(min(found), max(found) + 1)) if found else set()

def facing_sentences(attempt: Attempt, shapes: list[dict], aspect: float) -> dict:
    """By label, the sentence saying which way the piece's seats
    face in the photo, worked out only from its shape (photo pixels: x scaled
    by `aspect`; facing_fronts). An L by its long part; a corner sofa one
    clause per part; a curved sofa by its band. An L or corner sofa then says
    which end its second part is at (second_part_sentence). A piece
    whose black line is within FACING_PARALLEL_DEG of an earlier piece's,
    facing the same way, also says it stands parallel to it."""
    pieces = {(p["item"].id, p["sub_index"]): p for p in attempt_pieces(attempt)}
    out, seen = {}, []  # seen: (number, line angle, facing) of the pieces before
    for shape in shapes:
        piece = pieces.get((shape["item_id"], shape["sub_index"]))
        if piece is None:
            continue
        fronts = facing_fronts(shape, aspect, 1.0)
        facing = _front_of(fronts[0])
        bucket, side = facing
        if shape["kind"] == "corner":
            other = _front_of(fronts[1])
            sentence = (
                f"The long part's seats {FACING_PHRASES[bucket].format(side=side)}; "
                f"the shorter part's seats {FACING_PHRASES[other[0]].format(side=other[1])}."
            )
        else:
            sentence = FACING_SENTENCES[bucket].format(side=side)
        if shape["kind"] in ("L", "corner"):
            sentence += " " + second_part_sentence(shape, aspect)
        angle = _line_angle(fronts[0]["line"])
        number = piece["tag"].split(" · ")[0]
        for earlier, earlier_angle, earlier_facing in seen:
            gap = abs(angle - earlier_angle)
            if min(gap, 180 - gap) <= FACING_PARALLEL_DEG and earlier_facing == facing:
                sentence += f" It stands parallel to sofa {earlier}."
                break
        seen.append((number, angle, facing))
        out[piece["tag"]] = sentence
    return out

def second_part_sentence(shape: dict, aspect: float) -> str:
    """For an L-shaped or corner sofa, from its points in
    photo pixels, which end of the long part its lounge / shorter part is at
    as seen in the photo (LEFT / RIGHT; NEARER / FARTHER from the camera when
    the long part runs mostly up or down the photo), which way that part runs
    from the join, and for an L where its single arm is. No walls."""
    parts = [[(x * aspect, y) for x, y in part] for part in shape_parts(shape["kind"], shape["points"])]
    long_index = _long_part_index(shape, parts)
    second = 1 - long_index
    long_part = parts[long_index]
    joined, other = _edge_mid(long_part, CORNER_END_EDGE[long_index]), _edge_mid(long_part, FAR_END_EDGE[long_index])
    if abs(other[0] - joined[0]) >= abs(other[1] - joined[1]):
        end = "LEFT" if joined[0] < other[0] else "RIGHT"
        at, arm_at = f"at the {end} end of the long part", f"at the {'RIGHT' if end == 'LEFT' else 'LEFT'} end"
    elif joined[1] > other[1]:  # lower in the photo: nearer the camera
        at, arm_at = "at the end of the long part NEARER the camera", "at the end FARTHER from the camera"
    else:
        at, arm_at = "at the end of the long part FARTHER from the camera", "at the end NEARER the camera"
    from_join, tip = _edge_mid(parts[second], CORNER_END_EDGE[second]), _edge_mid(parts[second], FAR_END_EDGE[second])
    run = _run_words(tip[0] - from_join[0], tip[1] - from_join[1])
    if shape["kind"] == "L":
        return f"The lounge is {at}, as seen in the photo, and {run}. The single arm is {arm_at}."
    return f"The shorter part is {at}, as seen in the photo, and {run}."
